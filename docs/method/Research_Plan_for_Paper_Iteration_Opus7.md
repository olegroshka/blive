# Adversarial review and citation map for Cognitive Cartography

This report consolidates a systematic strengthening-and-attack review of *Cognitive Cartography: A Meta-Methodology for Human-AI Collaboration on Complex Problems* (Roshka, April 2026). The brief was clear: prioritise honest negative assessment over flattery. The headline finding is that **the paper occupies a real and currently under-occupied gap — domain-general, identifier-stable, lifecycle-managed substrate discipline for sustained human-LLM collaboration — but its central framing ("substrate matters at least as much as the model") makes a strong empirical claim with no empirical evidence, and it has not engaged the two literatures most likely to demolish it: Sutton's bitter-lesson tradition and the Polanyi/Suchman tacit-knowledge tradition.** Three concurrent 2026 preprints (Shift-Up, IACDM, ARIA) and the rapidly-canonised "context engineering" vocabulary mean the paper must also be repositioned more sharply than it currently is.

The recommended next iteration converts the paper from prescriptive ("here is the discipline") to **conditional and bounded** ("under bounded model context, no cross-session weight updates, human-auditability requirements, and mimeomorphic-dominant content, *some* externalised substrate helps; the specific six-category taxonomy is a Schelling point, not an empirical optimum; here are the falsifiers; here are the obsolescence conditions"). That conditional version is defensible. The unconditional version, as currently framed, is not.

## A. Strongest strengthening citations (12 essential additions)

Ranked by load-bearing value to the thesis.

**A1. Engelbart, D. C. (1962). *Augmenting Human Intellect: A Conceptual Framework*. SRI Project 3578, AFOSR-3223.** This is the **direct intellectual ancestor** of the paper's thesis and its absence is the single biggest historical-positioning gap. Engelbart's H-LAM/T system (Human using Language, Artifacts, and Methodology, in which he is Trained) maps almost 1:1 onto the paper's framework: artifacts = KB/ADR/OQ; methodology = the lifecycle and SSOT/DRY rules; language = the glossary. **Cite prominently in the introduction** with a phrasing like "We continue the Engelbart (1962) tradition of augmentation; where his substrate served a human, ours serves a human-AI dyad in which the AI's working memory is leaky between sessions."

**A2. Clark, A. & Chalmers, D. (1998). "The Extended Mind." *Analysis* 58(1):7–19.** DOI: 10.1093/analys/58.1.7. The canonical philosophical grounding for the substrate thesis. The parity principle and the Otto's-notebook example provide the *philosophical* warrant for treating KB-N/ADR-N/glossary entries as constitutive of cognition rather than merely supportive. **Cite in the foundational framing section alongside Hutchins 1995 and Scaife & Rogers 1996**, with a one-sentence bridge extending parity to human-AI dyads.

**A3. Hollan, J., Hutchins, E., & Kirsh, D. (2000). "Distributed cognition: toward a new foundation for human-computer interaction research." *ACM TOCHI* 7(2):174–196.** DOI: 10.1145/353485.353487. Operationalises distributed cognition as an HCI design framework and explicitly includes the *temporal* dimension (cognition distributed across time), which is essential for the paper's multi-session and restart-cost arguments. **Cite immediately after the existing Hutchins 1995 reference in the methodology section.**

**A4. Kirsh, D. & Maglio, P. (1994). "On Distinguishing Epistemic from Pragmatic Action." *Cognitive Science* 18(4):513–549.** DOI: 10.1207/s15516709cog1804_1. Provides the rigorous category — *epistemic action* — under which substrate maintenance is rational cognitive work, not procedural overhead. Updating an ADR or glossary entry is paradigmatically epistemic: it changes the world to simplify future cognition for both partners. **Cite in the section motivating why substrate maintenance is itself the work, not meta-work.**

**A5. Parnas, D. L. (1972). "On the Criteria To Be Used in Decomposing Systems into Modules." *CACM* 15(12):1053–1058.** DOI: 10.1145/361598.361623. The canonical citation for "the right decomposition is the one that localises change." This grounds the design choice that artefact identifiers (KB-N, ADR-N) are stable interfaces while their bodies churn — exactly Parnas's information-hiding principle applied to knowledge artefacts. **Cite in the substrate-ontology section** when defending stable identifiers and the glossary-as-semantic-anchor.

**A6. Evans, E. (2003). *Domain-Driven Design: Tackling Complexity in the Heart of Software*. Addison-Wesley.** This is **the most directly relevant SE precedent for "glossary as semantic anchor"** and the paper's lack of engagement with it is a glaring scholarship gap. Evans's *Ubiquitous Language* (rigorous shared vocabulary reflected in code, tests, and conversation, with mismatch treated as a defect) is precisely the discipline the paper proposes for the human-AI pair, and the Bounded Context concept justifies why each project gets its own KB. **Cite when defining the glossary artefact and when defending DRY-for-prose.**

**A7. Brooks, F. P. (1975/1995). *The Mythical Man-Month* — specifically the "Documentary Hypothesis" chapter.** Brooks's argument that **a small fixed set of formal documents must be the project's working tool, not a separate deliverable**, is the direct ancestor of the paper's six-category substrate. Pair with Brooks (1987) "No Silver Bullet" (IEEE Computer 20(4):10–19) for essential-vs-accidental complexity, which gives the paper its strongest rhetorical framing: the substrate attacks accidental complexity in human-AI collaboration (context loss, terminology drift, re-litigated decisions). **Cite in motivation and in the artefact-taxonomy chapter.**

**A8. Larkin, J. H. & Simon, H. A. (1987). "Why a Diagram is (Sometimes) Worth Ten Thousand Words." *Cognitive Science* 11(1):65–100.** Distinguishes informational equivalence from computational equivalence, providing the empirical-theoretical basis for "**the form of the substrate matters, not just its presence**." Justifies why structured artefacts (inventories, data dictionaries, ADR templates) outperform free prose containing the same facts. **Cite when motivating structured-artefact choices.** Pair with Zhang & Norman (1994) "Representations in Distributed Cognitive Tasks" (*Cognitive Science* 18(1):87–122).

**A9. Sumers, T. R. et al. (2024). "Cognitive Architectures for Language Agents" (CoALA). *TMLR*. arXiv:2309.02427.** This is the **single most important contemporary citation the paper currently lacks** — and engaging it is non-negotiable. CoALA already provides a principled, agent-managed memory taxonomy (working / episodic / semantic / procedural) grounded in cognitive science. The paper proposes a competing, human-managed taxonomy; failing to cite CoALA is a fatal scholarship gap. **Cite at the start of the substrate-formalisation section** and either (a) reframe the six categories as a *projection* of CoALA's memory types onto human-readable artefacts, or (b) explicitly argue that human-AI auditability requires human-prescribed structure that CoALA's internal memories cannot supply. Option (b) is the stronger move.

**A10. Anthropic Model Context Protocol (MCP, Nov 2024) plus Anthropic "Effective context engineering for AI agents" (Sept 2025) and "Effective harnesses for long-running agents" (Nov 2025).** anthropic.com/news/model-context-protocol; anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents. MCP is a deployed industry standard for "externalised, structured context" with broad cross-vendor adoption. Anthropic's "harness" piece names the **shift-engineer problem** — exactly the paper's restart-cost failure mode. **Cite as evidence that the substrate problem has crystallised into a protocol** and that the framing has industry-wide consensus.

**A11. Kuratov, Y. et al. (2024). "BABILong: Testing the Limits of LLMs with Long Context Reasoning-in-a-Haystack." NeurIPS 2024 D&B. arXiv:2406.10149.** Provides the paper's **single strongest empirical citation**: popular LLMs effectively utilise only **10–20% of their advertised context**, and RAG achieves a flat ~60% on single-fact QA independent of length. This is the most quotable evidence for "context windows postpone but do not dissolve failure modes." Pair with **HELMET (Yen et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv:2410.02694)** which shows NIAH does not predict downstream performance and that the open vs closed-source gap *widens* with length, and **NoCha (Karpinska et al., EMNLP 2024, arXiv:2406.16264)** which shows no open-weight model performs above random chance on book-length global reasoning. **Cite in the failure-modes section as the empirical backbone.**

**A12. Hsieh, C.-P. et al. (2024). "RULER: What's the Real Context Size of Your Long-Context Language Models?" COLM 2024. arXiv:2404.06654.** Plus **Wu, X. et al. (2025) "On the Emergence of Position Bias in Transformers" (ICML 2025, arXiv:2502.01951)**, which provides a **mechanistic graph-theoretic proof that causal masking creates position bias that strengthens with depth and cannot be eliminated without changing the masking**. This is the strongest theoretical citation for "you cannot train your way out of attention dilution" — pair with the existing Liu et al. 2024 reference. **Cite in the failure-modes section.**

**Vygotsky, L. S. (1978).** *Mind in Society* — the earliest rigorous statement of mediated cognition; cite in deep-history theoretical framing. **Norman, D. A. (1991). "Cognitive Artifacts"** in Carroll (ed.) — provides the canonical vocabulary "cognitive artifact" and the system/personal-view distinction (substrate doesn't make the model smarter; it changes the task the model is being asked to perform). **Bush, V. ## B. Strongest contradictory and tensional works (top 10)

These are ranked by severity of threat. The paper must engage every one of them; ignoring any of the top five would itself be a falsification-first failure.

**B1. Sutton, R. (2019). "The Bitter Lesson." incompleteideas.net.** *Threat: high, potentially fatal to the framing.* Sutton's argument — "general methods that leverage computation are ultimately the most effective" and "we should stop trying to find simple ways to think about the contents of minds… instead we should build in only the meta-methods that can find and capture this arbitrary complexity" — directly targets the paper's hand-engineered substrate taxonomy. The 2025 Sutton/Karpathy podcast exchange (Karpathy tweet ID 1973435013875314729; "summoning ghosts" framing) sharpens this: from Sutton's 2025 perspective, doubling down on human-curated artefacts is heading the wrong direction. **Engagement: concede the asymptotic case, narrow scope to the present transitional regime.** Add a dedicated *Sutton boundary condition* section that explicitly forecasts the paper's own obsolescence conditions: substrate-as-discipline is justified *only* while (a) agent context windows are bounded, (b) cross-session weight updates are unavailable to most users, and (c) agents cannot reliably maintain their own emergent substrate. Trying to rebut Sutton head-on will lose; explicitly bounding the claim is honest and defensible.

**B2. Sumers et al. (2024) CoALA + Wang et al. (2023) Voyager + the agentic-autonomy / self-organising memory wave (A-MEM, MemGPT, Generative Agents).** *Threat: high.* The 2023–2025 trajectory is unambiguously toward agent-managed, emergent substrate. Voyager's ever-growing skill library is the agent's own SSOT and ADR rolled into one — emergent, not prescribed. CoALA argues memory should be agent-managed. The paper's "append-only ADRs, status lifecycle, stable identifiers" reads like prescribed bureaucracy where the field is moving toward emergent self-organisation. **Engagement: differentiate on auditability grounds.** The strongest defensible claim is that *human auditability and oversight* require human-imposed structure even when the agent could self-organise. Make this a governance/epistemics argument, not an efficiency argument. Concede that Voyager-style emergent substrate may be more efficient in closed-world executable-feedback domains; claim the territory of human-in-the-loop, ambiguity-rich, multi-stakeholder work where audit trails are non-negotiable.

**B3. Polanyi, M. (1966). *The Tacit Dimension*.** *Threat: high; foundational philosophical objection.* "We can know more than we can tell." All knowledge has irreducibly tacit elements; attempts to make knowledge fully explicit fail in principle, not just in practice. The paper's hierarchical articulation discipline assumes complex projects *can* be hierarchically articulated. Polanyi's whole programme says this is false. Glossaries, ADRs, and Vision→Code chains capture explicit residue while the operative knowledge — "why we do it this way," "when to break the rule" — remains in human heads, irreducibly. **Engagement: concede explicitly and reframe.** Adopt a Polanyi-aware framing: "the substrate captures the externalisable fraction; the unexternalisable fraction remains the bottleneck and is what makes human-AI collaboration on truly complex problems hard." This actually *strengthens* the paper by avoiding overclaim. Failing to engage Polanyi signals the author hasn't faced the hardest philosophical case.

**B4. Suchman, L. A. (1987). *Plans and Situated Actions*.** *Threat: high.* Plans are resources for situated action, not specifications of it. Suchman demolished the AI-planning paradigm by showing humans construct meaning from local interactional contingencies, not pre-specified plans. The paper's Vision→Requirements→Design→Plan→Code→Tests→Operations forward propagation is a textbook AI-planning model that Suchman attacked in 1987. **Engagement: cite Suchman explicitly and reframe the propagation chain as providing *resources for situated action* (context, references, constraints), not a deterministic pipeline.** This is a substantive concession that strengthens the paper. Pair with **Dreyfus & Dreyfus (1986). *Mind Over Machine*** — the five-stage skill model implies that mandated articulation may regress experts to the "competent" rule-statable level, degrading expert performance. The paper has no current answer to "does the discipline degrade expert performance?" and that question will land hard with senior-engineer reviewers.

**B5. Brooks, F. P. (1987). "No Silver Bullet—Essence and Accident in Software Engineering." *IEEE Computer* 20(4):10–19.** *Threat: high.* "There is no single development… which by itself promises even one order of magnitude improvement." Methodology proposals have a poor empirical track record (CASE tools, formal methods, OO, agile-as-cure-all). SE-literate readers will weaponise Brooks against any methodology paper. **Engagement: explicitly disclaim order-of-magnitude productivity claims and exhibit Brooks-grade humility.** A short "no silver bullet" paragraph in the introduction inoculates against the most common reviewer reflex. Pair with **DeMarco & Lister, *Peopleware*** on the teamicide risk of capital-M Methodology and **Cockburn (2000) "Selecting a Project's Methodology" (*IEEE Software* 17(4):64–71)** on methodology weight matching project size and criticality. Note the irony that Cockburn is *already* cited (for hexagonal architecture) but his *adversarial* methodology-weight argument is not engaged — fix this.

**B6. Buchgeher, G. et al. (2023). "Using architecture decision records in open source projects — an MSR study on GitHub" (*Empirical Software Engineering*).** *Threat: medium-high; the citation that falsification-first orientation cannot afford to ignore.* MSR study of 900+ GitHub projects: ~50% of projects that initialise ADRs contain fewer than five records. **ADR adoption typically dies after a pilot phase.** **Engagement: face this finding head-on in the empirical landscape *and* the limitations section.** Position Cognitive Cartography explicitly as a *response* to this finding: the substrate plus AI enforcement (the LLM as the missing reader-of-prose) is the hypothesised mechanism for breaking the pilot-phase-abandonment pattern. State this as a falsifiable prediction. Pair with **Capilla et al. (2016) "10 years of software architecture knowledge management" (*JSS* 116:191–205)** on the burden-on-producer/benefit-to-consumer asymmetry and **Hata et al. (2019, ICSE) "9.6 Million Links in Source Code Comments"** on measurable link decay — both corroborate that documentation discipline does not stick on its own.

**B7. Long-context maximalism: RULER (Hsieh et al. 2024) GPT-4 holding 85% at 128K + Self-Route / Li et al. EMNLP 2024 "Retrieval Augmented Generation or Long-Context LLMs?" (arXiv:2407.16833).** *Threat: medium-high.* "When resourced sufficiently, LC consistently outperforms RAG in terms of average performance." This is the strongest single piece of evidence against externalised-substrate. Frontier models *do* handle long context reasonably well on retrieval-leaning synthetic tasks. **Engagement: cleanly distinguish task types.** On retrieval-from-context tasks, long context is genuinely improving; the paper is *not* about those. On multi-session, write-mode, reference-tracking, global-reasoning tasks, NoCha (55.8% best), LooGLE v2 (59.2% best), and LongBench v2 (50.1% non-CoT, barely above human-pressed 53.7%) show the maximalist case collapses. **Make this distinction explicitly in the abstract and §1.** Note the asymmetry the paper has not engaged: LLMs have million-token windows; *humans* still have Cowan's ~4-item working memory; the substrate exists primarily for human auditability, not for relieving model-side capacity.

**B8. Star, S. L. (2010). "This is Not a Boundary Object: Reflections on the Origin of a Concept." *STHV* 35(5):601–617.** *Threat: medium-high to a specific terminological move.* Star's own retrospective shows the concept of boundary objects has been used too elastically, conflating interpretive flexibility with the back-and-forth between ill- and well-structured uses. The paper currently cites Star & Griesemer 1989; this is incomplete. The paper's artefacts are likely *not* boundary objects in Star's sense — they are too well-structured, too prescriptive of the well-structured pole, and don't sit at a real social-world boundary. **Engagement: either engage Star (2010) explicitly and defend the usage, or rename the concept** (e.g., "coordination artefacts," "infrastructural records"). Pair with **Lee (2007). "Boundary Negotiating Artifacts." *CSCW* 16(3):307–339** — real collaboration is full of artefacts that *destabilise* protocols, and ordered append-only discipline assumes a routinised collaboration that ethnography shows is rare in genuinely innovative work.

**B9. Cognitive Load Theory critiques: de Jong (2010) *Instructional Science* 38(2):105–134; Schnotz & Kürschner (2007); plus Cowan (2001/2010) on the modern "magical four."** *Threat: medium.* If the paper invokes Sweller's CLT and Miller's 7±2 to motivate substrate, the foundation is shakier than the citations suggest: the three load types lack empirical separability; Sweller himself reformulated in 2010; the modern working-memory number is 3–5 (Cowan), not 7±2 (Miller); and **the capacity-limit argument applies to one side of the dyad only — frontier LLMs in 2026 have context capacity vastly exceeding any plausible substrate**. **Engagement: update Miller→Cowan; address the asymmetry head-on.** Reframe: substrate exists for *human auditability and shared semantic anchoring*, not capacity relief on the agent side. This is the more defensible argument anyway.

**B10. Model-level remediation: Bai et al. (2022) Constitutional AI; Rafailov et al. (2023) DPO; Lightman et al. (2023) "Let's Verify Step by Step" (PRMs); plus hallucination-reduction work (Tian et al. ICLR 2024; OPA-DPO 2025 showing 43.2% reduction with 2.4k samples).** *Threat: medium-high to the necessity claim.* The paper's implicit claim that substrate is *necessary* for hallucination/drift control is empirically contested. Many "phantom decision" symptoms are being reduced by training-time interventions without external substrate. **Engagement: drop the necessity claim; argue for complementarity.** Substrate plus model-level remediation > either alone. Distinguish *single-turn* alignment problems (model-side tractable) from *cross-session* coherence (still requires externalised state because the model has no cross-session memory by default). Concede that the substrate's necessity is a function of current model capability and will erode as PRMs, RLHF, and DPO improve.

### Honourable mentions worth a paragraph each
**Feyerabend (1975) *Against Method*** — the methodologist's hardest interlocutor; recommendation is to disavow "this is THE methodology" framing in favour of "one coherent articulation discipline; alternatives may work; what matters is *some* externalised substrate." **Beck/XP and the Agile Manifesto (2001)** — the strongest move available is to position Cognitive Cartography as **XP's documentation policy plus an automated reader who actually reads** (i.e., the LLM is the missing compiler-for-prose; READ/IDENTIFY-SSOT/IMPACT-CHECK is the truth-check XP couldn't have because the only reader was a busy human). Note that Stettina & Heijstek (2011, SIGDOC) found over half of agile developers want *more* documentation than they have, undercutting the "agile says no docs" misreading. **Boehm & Turner (2003) *Balancing Agility and Discipline*** — adopt their home-ground framing and explicitly locate Cognitive Cartography on their axes (size × criticality × dynamism × culture × personnel-skill plus a new "cross-session continuity required" axis). **AutoML / NAS counter-counter**: the paper can soften the AutoML-inevitability narrative by citing Yang et al. (2020) and follow-ups showing many NAS papers don't beat random-search baselines once properly evaluated — useful nuance, not a full rebuttal.

## C. Adjacent framings and direct competitors

The space is **crowded with adjacent work but sparse with direct competitors**. The four pieces the paper *must* engage explicitly are listed first.

**C1. "Context engineering" as canonised vocabulary (Karpathy, Lutke, Willison, Schmid, Lance Martin, Mei et al. 2025 survey arXiv:2507.13334).** *This is the highest-overlap adjacent framing and any reviewer will know the term.* **Differentiation strategy:** unit of analysis differs (inference call vs project lifetime); audience differs (engineers building LLM products vs practitioners using LLMs on a single complex problem); mechanism differs (dynamic context-assembly pipelines vs human-curated, identifier-stable, append-only artefacts); epistemic stance differs (systems-engineering optimisation vs epistemic discipline). Recommended exact phrasing: *"Context engineering tells you what to put in the window; Cognitive Cartography tells you what to keep outside the window so that any window can be re-assembled coherently across sessions, sub-agents, and even model generations."* The abstract and §1 must inoculate against the pattern-match.

**C2. Moreira (March 2026). "IACDM: Interactive Adversarial Convergence Development Methodology." arXiv:2604.16399.** *The most direct concurrent competitor; failure to engage is a fatal scholarship gap.* IACDM is also tool-agnostic, also targets the verification gap, and explicitly emphasises "deep problem discovery, persistent knowledge management across sessions, and systematic adversarial critique." **Differentiation:** IACDM's three pillars are *process* pillars; Cognitive Cartography's six categories are *artefact* categories with stable identifiers and lifecycle. IACDM has no SSOT/DRY-for-prose, no append-only ADR with supersedes, no glossary-as-semantic-anchor, no MISSING→DRAFT→STABLE→STALE lifecycle. IACDM is positioned as an SE methodology; Cognitive Cartography is a meta-methodology for any complex problem. **Cite as concurrent work.**

**C3. Belzner, Gabor & Wirsing variants — "Shift-Up" framework (arXiv:2604.20436, Jan 2026).** Uses *executable requirements (BDD), C4 modelling, and ADRs* as structural guardrails for GenAI-native development. **Differentiation:** Shift-Up adapts ADRs and BDD specifically for code-generation agents in SE; Cognitive Cartography generalises to any multi-session AI collaboration with six artefact categories and stable identifiers beyond ADRs. Position Shift-Up as a confirming instance from the SE specialisation.

**C4. Spec-Driven Development family: Piskala (Feb 2026) arXiv:2602.00180; Marri (Jan 2026) "Constitutional Spec-Driven Development" arXiv:2602.02584; GitHub Spec Kit; ARIA arXiv:2510.11143.** **Differentiation:** SDD treats one artefact (the spec) as authoritative SSOT; Cognitive Cartography says SSOT is a *category property* applied differentially across six artefact types. SDD has no OQ-N (open questions queue is a SDD blind spot), no glossary-as-semantic-anchor, no append-only-with-supersedes ADR semantics.

### Other adjacent framings (paragraph each)
**Forte (2022) *Building a Second Brain*** — closest popular adjacency in spirit; differentiate by audience (PKM for individual reading vs project substrate for a human-AI dyad) and mechanism (no decision records, OQ queue, glossary-as-anchor, session warm-up). Recommended phrasing: *"Forte's Second Brain is a personal knowledge archive optimised for human re-reading; Cognitive Cartography is a project substrate optimised for re-loading an AI collaborator's working context."* **Mollick (2024) *Co-Intelligence*** — the cultural/managerial framing; differentiate as *"Mollick describes the attitude; this paper describes the artefacts."* **Karpathy "LLM as new operating system"** — differentiate by orthogonality (LLM OS is about how machines will be architected; Cognitive Cartography is what a user puts on the filesystem of any LLM OS). **Anthropic "Building Effective Agents" (Schluntz & Zhang Dec 2024)** — differentiate by scope (within-run agentic patterns vs across-session human-AI collaboration). **Schulhoff et al. (2024) "The Prompt Report" (arXiv:2406.06608)** — differentiate cleanly: prompting techniques optimise a single inference; Cognitive Cartography organises the project state against which many such inferences are run. **Andrew Ng "Agentic Workflows" (2024)** — Ng's four patterns (Reflection, Tool use, Planning, Multi-agent) are intra-task design patterns; Cognitive Cartography is across-task and human-side. **Kelly (1955) PCT and Glasersfeld (1995) Radical Constructivism** — one paragraph in related work as the epistemological precedent; the paper is not a contribution to constructivist theory but an operationalisation of constructivist commitments for human-AI dyads. **Process methodologies (PMBOK, Kanban, Scrum, SAFe)** — one paragraph; Cognitive Cartography is artefact-discipline, process-agnostic, AI-collaborator-specific. **Cognition AI's "Don't Build Multi-Agents" (Walden Yan, June 2025)** vs **Anthropic's multi-agent research system (June 2025)** — cite both; the unresolved debate maps to read-mode vs write-mode tasks; Cognitive Cartography lives on the write-mode side.

## D. Empirical gaps and reviewer-anticipation analysis

**The paper has no empirical evaluation, and this is its most exposed flank.** Methodology-paper reviewers at top venues will not accept a single self-applied case as evidence. The honest reviewer-anticipation map looks like this.

**Methodological standards reviewers will apply.** Yin (2018, 6th ed.) *Case Study Research and Applications* — single-case studies are legitimate when critical, unusual, common, revelatory, or longitudinal, but require explicit case-selection rationale, unit of analysis, multiple evidence sources, chain of evidence, and rival-explanation discussion. Runeson & Höst (2009) "Guidelines for conducting and reporting case study research in software engineering" (*EMSE* 14(2):131–164) — the ICSE/EMSE/IEEE Software gold standard; demands explicit construct/internal/external/reliability validity discussion and a case study protocol. Ralph et al. (2020) "Empirical Standards for Software Engineering Research" (arXiv:2010.03525, ACM SIGSOFT living standards) — for "Methodology" or "Engineering Research" papers, demands clear problem statement, artefact description, justified design decisions, demonstrated utility (example, prototype, case study, or controlled experiment), and explicit limitations. Hevner et al. (2004) "Design Science in Information Systems Research" (*MIS Quarterly* 28(1):75–105) and Wieringa (2014) *Design Science Methodology* — the framing most natural for this paper; following the seven Hevner guidelines (especially evaluation and rigour) materially improves reception.

**Reviewer objections to anticipate.** *(i) "Why exactly six artefact categories?"* The paper needs an explicit derivation argument or honest acknowledgement that the six are empirically derived from the author's practice and offered as a starting taxonomy, not an axiomatic decomposition. *(ii) "Single self-applied case is the weakest empirical position possible."* Even one independent case (a colleague applying the methodology) materially strengthens the paper. *(iii) "Domain-agnostic claim with software-only examples."* The strongest test is a non-software case study (research-paper writing, technical-policy analysis, complex investigation) — without one, the domain-agnostic claim is weak. *(iv) "What would falsify this?"* Given the author's stated falsification-first stance, the paper *must* commit to operational predictions. A defensible move: state that a controlled study where Cognitive Cartography users show no improvement on multi-session task coherence metrics relative to context-engineering-only users would falsify the central claim; specify the metrics in advance. *(v) "Why isn't this just context engineering?"* Inoculate in the abstract; expand in §1. *(vi) "Why is this not CoALA reframed?"* Engage CoALA explicitly in §2 or §3. *(vii) "How does this relate to IACDM, Shift-Up, SDD, ARIA?"* Cite all four as concurrent work; differentiate cleanly.

**Venue map (frank).** **Likely reject without empirical study: CHI, CSCW (PACM HCI), TOCHI, ICSE research track, TOSEM, EMSE.** A pure prescriptive methodology paper with one author's self-application will not clear these venues' bars. **Best primary targets in current form: IEEE Software** (peer-reviewed magazine, methodology-friendly, ~5,000 words; the single best fit, with a long history of accepting methodology proposals such as Nygard's ADRs); **ACM Queue** (practitioner essay venue, opinionated methodology pieces welcome); **CACM** (Viewpoint or Contributed Article; high prestige, editorial gatekeeping). **Best secondary targets: ICSE NIER** (4-page early-ideas), **ICSE SEIP** (industrial experience report), **JSS** (with modest empirical extension), **CHASE workshop** (Cooperative and Human Aspects of SE; explicitly welcomes human-aspects methodology). **Workshop strategy first**: submit a 6–8 page version to AI4SE @ ICSE 2026, Tools for Thought @ CHI 2026, or CHASE 2026 to gather feedback before targeting a magazine/journal article. **Critical lift**: if 5–15 practitioners can be recruited to apply the methodology over 4–8 weeks with structured pre/post interviews and artefact analysis, **CSCW (PACM HCI) and TOSEM become realistic**, with substantially more citation impact than a magazine.

**Naming risk.** "Cognitive Cartography" is evocative but does not signal the SE/HCI audience clearly. Reviewers searching by keyword may miss it. The subtitle should include explicit terms: *human-AI collaboration*, *methodology*, *substrate*, *knowledge artefacts*. Consider testing the title against arXiv search results for "context engineering," "agentic methodology," "AI-native software engineering" to make sure the paper surfaces.

## E. Ranked list of the most important changes for the next iteration

These are the seven changes that, in priority order, would most improve the paper's defensibility, scholarship, and reception.

**1. Convert from unconditional to conditional framing.** Add a Sutton boundary-condition section that explicitly states: substrate-as-discipline is justified *only* while (a) agent context windows are bounded, (b) cross-session weight updates are unavailable to most users, (c) agents cannot reliably maintain their own emergent substrate, and (d) human auditability is required. State the obsolescence conditions plainly. Drop the strong "matters at least as much as the model" framing in favour of "matters in conjunction with the model under conditions X." This is the single highest-leverage change and it is forced by Sutton (B1), CoALA/Voyager (B2), and the model-level remediation literature (B10). Without this change the paper is broadside-vulnerable.

**2. Engage CoALA (Sumers et al. 2024) prominently, plus Polanyi and Suchman.** The current scholarship gap on CoALA is fatal — it is the most important contemporary work on agent memory architectures and proposes a *competing taxonomy*. Either reframe the six categories as a projection of CoALA's cognitive-science memory types onto human-readable artefacts, or argue (preferred) that human auditability requires human-prescribed structure that CoALA's internal memories cannot supply. Simultaneously, engage Polanyi's tacit-dimension critique and Suchman's plans-vs-situated-actions critique — concede that the substrate captures the externalisable fraction only, and reframe the Vision→Code propagation chain as resources for situated action rather than a deterministic pipeline. These three engagements together convert the paper from naively prescriptive to philosophically self-aware.

**3. Add empirical content, even minimal.** A single self-applied case is the weakest possible empirical position. The minimum viable improvement: recruit three to five colleagues to apply the methodology to genuinely independent projects over 4–8 weeks with structured pre/post interviews and artefact-analysis. The stretch goal: a controlled comparison against context-engineering-only baselines. State explicit falsifiers in advance (per the author's falsification-first orientation): the central claim is falsified if Cognitive Cartography users show no improvement on operationally-defined multi-session task-coherence metrics. **Without empirical content, top-tier venues (CHI, CSCW, ICSE, TOSEM) will reject; IEEE Software, ACM Queue, and CACM remain viable.** Pair with a candid threats-to-validity section using Runeson & Höst's structure, and frame the paper explicitly as Design Science Research per Hevner et al. (2004) to give SE/IS reviewers a known evaluation lens.

**4. Sharpen positioning against the four direct/closest neighbours: context engineering (Karpathy/Mei survey), IACDM (Moreira March 2026), Shift-Up (Jan 2026), SDD (Piskala/ARIA 2026).** Cite all four prominently. The differentiation must be sharp and stated in the abstract: *"Context engineering tells you what to put in the window; Cognitive Cartography tells you what to keep outside the window so any window can be re-assembled coherently."* IACDM has process pillars; Cognitive Cartography has artefact categories with stable identifiers. Shift-Up adapts ADRs for code-generation agents; Cognitive Cartography generalises across domains. SDD treats one artefact as SSOT; Cognitive Cartography says SSOT is a category property applied across six. The paper must also briefly differentiate from Forte's *Building a Second Brain*, Mollick's *Co-Intelligence*, Anthropic's *Building Effective Agents*, and Andrew Ng's agentic workflows.

**5. Engage the most damaging empirical findings honestly.** Buchgeher et al. (2023): ~50% of ADR-adopting projects have fewer than five records — adoption dies after pilot phase. The paper should treat this as the central empirical motivation for the LLM-as-enforcer hypothesis and state the falsifiable prediction that substrate-plus-AI-enforcement breaks the abandonment pattern. RULER GPT-4 holds 85% at 128K + Self-Route LC ≥ RAG: distinguish task types cleanly (retrieval-from-context vs multi-session write-mode). Capilla et al. (2016) on producer-burden/consumer-benefit asymmetry, Hata et al. (2019, ICSE) on link decay, and Aghajani et al. (2020, ICSE) on time-pressure as the dominant cause of doc rot — all should appear in the empirical-landscape section to demonstrate the author has faced the documentation-skeptic literature head-on.

**6. Strengthen the foundational citation graph at both ends of history.** Add Engelbart (1962) as the direct intellectual ancestor in the introduction; Clark & Chalmers (1998) as the philosophical grounding; Hollan-Hutchins-Kirsh (2000) for the temporal dimension of distributed cognition; Kirsh & Maglio (1994) for epistemic action; Parnas (1972) for information-hiding; Evans (2003) DDD for ubiquitous-language as direct ancestor of glossary-as-anchor (this is currently a glaring gap); Brooks's "Documentary Hypothesis" chapter and "No Silver Bullet"; Larkin & Simon (1987) plus Zhang & Norman (1994) for representation-determinism. At the contemporary end: BABILong, HELMET, NoCha, and Wu et al. (2025) ICML for the architectural inevitability of position bias; Anthropic MCP and "harnesses for long-running agents" plus Karpathy's June 2025 context-engineering tweets to anchor vocabulary in current consensus; ACE (Zhang et al. Oct 2025) as the most important 2025 empirical result on engineered-context matching weight-updating. Drop weaker candidates (Ahrens 2017, Norman *Design of Everyday Things*) in favour of better academic alternatives (Schmidt 2018 on Luhmann's card index; Norman 1991 *Cognitive Artifacts* chapter).

**7. Address the model-vs-human asymmetry the paper currently glosses.** Update Miller→Cowan on working memory; acknowledge that frontier LLMs in 2026 have context capacity vastly exceeding any plausible substrate, so the capacity-relief argument applies to the *human* side of the dyad only. Reframe the substrate's primary function as **shared semantic anchoring and human auditability**, not capacity relief on the agent side. This is both more defensible and more honest. It also turns the agentic-autonomy critique (B2) from a body blow into a clean differentiation: emergent agent-side substrate may be more efficient for agent-only consumption, but it cannot serve the human auditability function that Cognitive Cartography is for.

The pattern across these seven changes is a single move: **drop the strongest claims, gain the strongest defences.** The conditional, philosophically self-aware, empirically-motivated version of Cognitive Cartography is a publishable, citable, and defensible contribution. The unconditional version, as currently framed, is not.
